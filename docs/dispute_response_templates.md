# Dispute response templates — admin reference

Internal reference for handling inbound dispute / complaint / report
emails to `admin@canvascircle.art`. Copy/paste the matching template,
edit the bracketed placeholders, send.

Keep responses short. Always cite the policy page; don't litigate the
rule itself. The single best signal of platform health is consistency,
not having every angry sender agree with you.

The 24-hour cooling-off rule applies: log the report on receipt, but
take no account-level action within the first 24 hours. Reply with the
appropriate acknowledgement template the same day; act (or decline to
act) the next.

---

## 1. Transaction dispute — buyer vs. seller (most common)

Use when a buyer emails about an issue with a specific transaction:
condition disagreement, slow shipping, miscommunication, "they ghosted
me," partial-refund disputes, authenticity disagreements, "they didn't
tell me X."

```
Hi [name],

Thanks for reaching out. I'm sorry the transaction with [seller @handle]
hasn't gone the way you expected.

CanvasCircle doesn't mediate transactions between buyers and sellers —
we have no payment, escrow, or shipping infrastructure to act on
individual sales. The recourse for issues like this is the payment
platform you used:

  • PayPal Goods & Services — open a dispute within their stated window
    for "item not as described" or "item not received" claims
  • Venmo Goods & Services — file under "Get help" in the app
  • For high-value items, a third-party authentication service or
    small-claims court in the seller's jurisdiction are options
  • The seller's accepted References are Established Members who
    publicly vouched for them. You can message a reference directly
    and explain the situation; they may revoke their vouch if there's
    a clear pattern.

We do log reports of this kind. If a pattern emerges across multiple
buyers — multiple independent complaints with evidence pointing at the
same seller — that escalates to an account-level review under our
[Dispute Policy](https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html#suspension-rubric).
A single complaint without a pattern doesn't.

Hope you get it sorted out.

— CanvasCircle admin
```

## 2. "Ban this person" / general account complaint

Use when a user asks the admin to suspend or ban another user. Often
triggered by personal grievance or transaction frustration. The reply
sets expectations without dismissing the report.

```
Hi [name],

Thanks for the report — it's logged.

A few things worth being upfront about:

We evaluate reports on evidence, not on volume or relationship. One
complaint isn't sufficient for account-level action; a pattern of
independent reports with supporting evidence is. If you have screenshots,
messages, or specific listings to point at, please reply with them.

If this is rooted in a specific transaction (shipping, condition, price,
authenticity), CanvasCircle doesn't mediate those — see the
[Transaction dispute recourse](https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html#transaction-recourse)
section of our Dispute Policy. Your payment provider (PayPal G&S, Venmo
G&S) is the right path for that type of issue.

If the concern is genuinely account-level — fake listings, banned-
credential signups, falsified verification videos, harassment, etc. —
the [suspension rubric](https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html#suspension-rubric)
documents what we act on and what the evidence threshold looks like.

I'll review your report under our 24-hour cooling-off practice and reach
out only if action is taken on it. Most reports of this kind don't
trigger immediate action, which is intentional — it keeps the platform
from over-reacting to one-off concerns.

— CanvasCircle admin
```

## 3. Rule complaint / user upset about policy

Use when a user disagrees with a CanvasCircle rule itself (verification
video requirement, fine-art definition, collectors-only policy, etc.).
Don't litigate the rule. Don't apologize for having a rule. Decline
politely.

```
Hi [name],

Our policies are documented at:

  • https://canvascircle.art/guidelines.html
  • https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html
  • https://canvascircle.art/terms.html

We apply them consistently — we don't make exceptions on a per-user
basis, because exceptions erode the structure for everyone. You're
welcome to keep using the platform within those rules, or to use
platforms with different rules. Both are reasonable choices.

If you believe a specific decision was applied incorrectly (rather than
disagreeing with the rule itself), our
[Appeals process](https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html#appeals)
covers that — reply with new information and the decision gets a
second look.

— CanvasCircle admin
```

## 4. Suspension or ban notification (outbound from CanvasCircle)

Use when notifying an account that has been suspended or banned.
Always cite the specific policy section. Always include the appeal
path.

```
Hi [name / @handle],

This is to inform you that your CanvasCircle account has been
[suspended for N days / permanently banned] under our Dispute Policy.

Reason: [specific section from the suspension rubric — e.g.
"Falsified ownership-verification video" or "Pattern of misrepresentation
across multiple verified buyer reports"]

Evidence considered: [brief, factual list — e.g. "Three independent
buyer reports filed between [date] and [date], each citing condition
issues consistent with the [pattern]." DO NOT include personal
commentary or judgment about the user.]

Policy reference:
https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html#suspension-rubric

If you believe this decision was applied incorrectly, you can appeal
once. Reply to this email with any new information that wasn't
available at the time of the decision. We review appeals against the
same rubric — if new evidence changes the assessment, the action is
reversed; if not, the decision stands and the appeal is closed.

Continued back-and-forth after a closed appeal isn't engaged with —
the policy is at the link above and applies the same way to every
account.

— CanvasCircle admin
```

## 5. Appeal response (after suspension/ban appeal)

Use when responding to an appeal. Either reverses or confirms. No
middle ground; no extended back-and-forth.

### Reversal

```
Hi [name / @handle],

Thanks for the additional information. Reviewing the original
decision against [the new evidence], the action has been reversed.

Your account is restored to [previous status]. Apologies for the
disruption.

— CanvasCircle admin
```

### Confirmation

```
Hi [name / @handle],

Thanks for the appeal. The original decision stands — the new
information doesn't change the assessment against our
[suspension rubric](https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html#suspension-rubric).

Specifically: [one-sentence reason citing the rubric section]

This appeal is closed. Per the Dispute Policy, we don't engage further
on closed appeals.

— CanvasCircle admin
```

## 6. Bad-faith / personal-attack messages

Use when a user goes scorched-earth — name-calling, threats, repeated
incoherent complaints, public personal attacks. **DO NOT REPLY** if it's
purely abusive. Silence is policy. If a response is required (e.g. they
filed a legitimate-looking report buried in the rant):

```
Hi [name],

The substance of your report has been logged and will be reviewed
under the standard process at
https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html

The tone of your message is noted. Continued abusive communication is
itself a violation of our policy under
https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html#suspension-rubric
("Harassment of other users") and may result in account-level action.

— CanvasCircle admin
```

After this single response, don't engage further. Mark the thread.
If it continues, take the cooling-off-then-act path.

## 7. Reporting acknowledgement (boilerplate auto-reply)

Use when ANY report comes in — set expectations on response time and
the standard process. Send within a few hours, then act under the
24-hour rule.

```
Hi [name],

Thanks — your report has been received and logged.

Standard practice on CanvasCircle is a 24-hour cooling-off period
before any account-level action, which is intentional and applies
even to clear-cut cases (it prevents reactive over-correction).

You generally won't be notified of what action (if any) is taken,
since that's between CanvasCircle and the reported account. If we need
additional information from you, we'll reach out.

Our full Dispute Policy is at
https://canvascircle.art/dispute-policy.html — it documents what we
will and won't act on, what evidence threshold matters, and the appeal
process.

— CanvasCircle admin
```

## Tone meta-rules (apply across all templates)

1. **Don't apologize for the policy.** "I'm sorry we can't help" only,
   not "I'm sorry we have this rule."
2. **Cite, don't litigate.** Every position links to the policy page.
   You're not the rule, you're the implementer of the rule.
3. **Never reference your personal opinion of the user.** All decisions
   reference rules + evidence; never "I think you're being unreasonable."
4. **Short over long.** Long emails invite long replies. The shorter
   and more cite-driven your response, the faster the conversation
   converges.
5. **Don't engage in DMs about closed cases.** Once an appeal is closed,
   it's closed. Repeated messages get no response. This is exhausting if
   you let it continue.
6. **The 24-hour rule is for YOU.** Even if you're 100% sure of a
   decision, sleep on it. Re-read the policy the next day before
   acting. This single discipline prevents most regret-bans.
