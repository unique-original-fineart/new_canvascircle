When Guy asks "let me know what you think" or "is this the right approach", he genuinely wants a recommendation — not a neutral enumeration of options for him to pick from blind.

**Why:** He made better decisions when I flagged real tradeoffs and committed to a recommendation. Example: he asked for a 3-up mobile catalog grid; I built it but flagged that cards would be ~110px wide and microscopic; he reconsidered, asked for my opinion, I recommended 2-up; he agreed and that's what shipped. If I'd just built 3-up without the pushback, it would've felt cramped and we'd have had to redo it.

**How to apply:** When a clear better option exists, lead with the recommendation and explain the tradeoff briefly. Pushback is welcome when his instinct conflicts with what would actually work better — frame it as "here's what I'd do and why" rather than just "here are some options." Don't hedge to seem agreeable.
